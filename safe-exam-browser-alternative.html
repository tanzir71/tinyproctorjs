Safe Exam Browser

What Safe Exam Browser does

Safe Exam Browser (SEB) is a free, open-source lockdown application for Windows, macOS and iOS. It opens the exam in kiosk mode, blocks app switching and system shortcuts, and integrates with Moodle, ILIAS, OpenOLAT and several commercial exam systems. It's the strongest free prevention tool available.

tinyproctor.js

How tinyproctor.js differs

tinyproctor.js is also free and open source, but solves the opposite half of the problem: telemetry instead of lockdown, zero install instead of managed configuration. The two are complementary — SEB prevents what it can, tinyproctor.js records what happened. For cohorts where installing SEB is impractical (BYOD, remote, mobile), tinyproctor.js is the lighter-weight option.

Which should you choose?

Choose Safe Exam Browser if…

you control the devices (computer labs, managed laptops) or your students can reliably install software, and prevention matters more than telemetry.

Choose tinyproctor.js if…

installs are impossible or risky for your cohort, you need an audit trail of what happened during attempts, or you want monitoring on mobile devices. Many teams run both: SEB where they can, tinyproctor.js everywhere else.

Frequently asked questions

More answers in the docs FAQ.

Are both tools really free?

Yes. Safe Exam Browser is open source under MPL; tinyproctor.js is open source under MIT. Neither charges per student or per exam.

Can I use tinyproctor.js inside Safe Exam Browser?

Yes. SEB renders normal web pages, so the script runs fine inside it — giving you SEB's prevention plus tinyproctor's event timeline in one setup.

Which is more secure?

SEB. Kiosk-mode lockdown is categorically stronger than in-page JavaScript. tinyproctor.js trades some enforcement strength for zero-install deployment and server-side telemetry.
